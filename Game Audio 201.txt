Section 3: Stereo Recording Techniques, Phase, BIG Combat Sounds
- Phase
-- A particular point in the cycle of a waveform, measured as an angle.
-- Plays a huge role when combining waveforms.
-- Interference
--- The way in which two waveforms interact
--- Positive Interference
---- An increase in amplitude.
--- Negative Interference
---- A decrease in amplitude.
--- Interference is negligible in waveforms that are not nearly identical.
--- Complex waveforms make the effects more timbrel than volume.
-- Phase and Interference in the DAW and Studio
--- DAW sources:
---- Duplicating a track
---- Polarity switch. Cancels a sound.
---- Delay.
---- Parallel Processing
--- Studio Sources:
---- Two mics picking up the same source (3:1 rule)
---- Standing Waves
---- Incorrect polarity of speakers and mics.
--- 3:1 Rule
---- When recording with multiple mics, the second mic must be at least 3 times the distance away from a sound source than the first mic.
---- Exceptions:
----- Stereo Pair (Treat the pair as a single mic)
----- Drum Set
- Stereo Recording Techniques
-- Two mics can provide us with a stereo recording.
-- Must be set up to avoid phase cancellation issues.
-- Stereo Microphone: Has two transducers that record in stereo.
-- Stereo Pair: Two Microphones, typically of the same model, set up to record in stereo.
-- Matched Pair: Two microphones of the same model with adjacent serial numbers meant to be used together as a stereo pair.
-- X/Y
--- Two coincident cardioid mic capsules at an angle of 90 degrees
--- Smallest stereo image with the least phase cancellation and the most mono compatibility.
--- Use in a bad sounding room or if end user will likely have a system that plays back in mono or close to it
-- ORTF
--- Two capsules 17 cm apart at a 110 degree angle.
--- Simulates placement of ears, similar to Binaural.
--- Wider image than X/Y
--- Little phase cancellation and good mono compatibility.
--- Use in nicer sounding rooms and for large groups.
-- Blumlein Pair
--- Two coincident, crossed, figure 8 capsules.
--- Wider image than X/Y.
--- Little Phase Cancellation and good mono compatibility.
--- Lots of Room Sound.
--- Use for very natural sounding stereo recording in good sound rooms.
-- Spaced Pair
--- Two Omni mics spaced 3-10ft
--- Wide Stereo image
--- Suffers from phase cancellation, little mono compatibility, sometimes lacks center energy.
--- Use to supplement close miked sources in a good sounding room.
-- M/S (Mid/Side)
--- Two coincident mic capsules
--- One Cardioid, facing forward.
--- One figure 8, facing to the sides.
--- Cardioid picks up mono sound, figure 8 picks up stereo.
--- Requires Mid/Side Matrix processing.
--- Allows for variable stereo imaging.
--- 100% mono compatible.
--- Can sound weird but cool.
-- Binaural
--- Two Cadioid mic capsules in the ears of a dummy head.
--- Dummy head provides acoustic separation between mics and creates phase cancellation similar to human hearing.
--- Sound hyper-realistic in headphones.
--- Sounds terrible in speakers.
--- Little Mono compatibility.
--- Can simulate surround sounds but they're expensive.

Voice/Dialogue Recording and Editing
- Decibels and Metering
-- Humans are sensitive to a very wide range of pressure amplitudes.
-- 20 pascals is the quietest we can hear.
-- 63,000 pascals is the threshold of pain
-- Like pitch, we perceive loudness logarithmically.
-- A sound is perceives as twice as loud when its power is 10x as large (power is different from pressure)
-- This makes communicating about loudness hard.
- Decibels makes things easier!
-- Converts large numbers to smaller ones, achieved with a logarithm.
-- A comparison between a reference value and another value, achieved with a ratio.
-- Has no physical quantity.
-- Closely related to the way humans perceive loudness.
-- dB = 10log(power2/power1)
- Important Decibel Changes
-- 0dB: No change
-- 3dB: Doubling in power/acoustic energy. Smallest loudness change that an untrained ear will hear.
-- 6dB: Quadrupling in power/acoustic energy, doubling in pressure.
-- 10db: 10 times the power, double in perceived loudness.
- Common Reference Values
-- dBSPL: 0db = 20 pascals, the threshold of hearing. Used in sound pressure meters.
-- dBV/dBu: 0dB = nominal operating voltage level of analog system. Is comfortably audible. Used on analog gear.
-- dBFS (dB Full Scale): 0dB = point of distortion in digital gear. Causes all dB measures to have negative numbers.
- Importance of Level Meters
-- Amplifiers and speakers can be turned up and down.
-- A level meter is your best reference for how loud something is.
-- Different types of meters can give us different important information about the mix.
- Peak vs RMS values
-- Loudness of complex waveforms changes rapidly.
-- And dB measurement is affected by how long the measurement is being taken.
-- Peak Values: Measurements taken nearly instantly.
-- RMS(root mean square) Values: Measurements taken more slowly and the transients are averaged in.
-- RMS measurements are always lower than the peak measurements of the same material.
- Meter Types
-- Peak Meter: Displays peak measurements. Best for normal use in digital applications.
-- RMS Meter: Displays RMS measurements. Use to meter average loudness of a mix.
-- VU Meter: specific RMS meter that displays measurements in Volume Units. Used on analog consoles.
- Dialogue Editing and RX 7
-- Try to make cuts at areas at Unity.

Delay and Modulation Effects
- Delay
-- Delaying the start of the sound.
-- When blended with the non-delayed sound, it creates and echo effect.
-- The time between the dry signal and the delayed signal helps determine the perceived size of the space.
-- The volume difference between the dry signal and the wet signal determines how far away the source is perceived.
- Delay Controls
-- Delay Time: How long the wet signal is delayed (changing this in real time causes pitch shift!)
-- Mix: balance between the wet and dry signal.
-- Feedback: How much of the delayed signals is fed back into the processor. Creates repeated echoes.
-- Rate: How quickly the delay time is modulated.
-- Depth: How widely the delay time is modulated.
- Reverb
-- In acoustics, the blend of echoes that a space creates when a sound is present in it.
-- The louder the wet signal, as compared to the dry signal, the further away the source seems.
-- The more time between the dry and wet signal, the larger the space seems.
-- The longer the reverb, the bigger, emptier, and harder-surfaced the space seems.
- Reverb Controls
-- Decay: How long the reverb takes to decay
-- Pre Delay: Time before the reverb begins.
-- Diffusion: How close together the initial echoes in the delay effect are (important for percussive sounds)
-- Mix: Balance between the wet signal and the dry signal.
-- Some reverbs have EQ and advanced early reflections controls as well.
- LFO
-- Something used to control another parameter.
- Flanger
-- A very short delayed signal is blended with the dry signal causing comb filtering.
-- Comb Filtering: Constructive and destructive interference pattern resulting from a complex waveform interfering and itself.
-- The short delay is modulated
-- As the delay is changed, the comb filter pattern changes, causing a whoosh sound.
- Flanger Controls
-- Rate: How quick the delay time changes.
-- Depth: How much the delay time changes.
-- Feedback: How much of the flanged signal is looped back in for more flanging.
- Chorus
-- The same as flanger but with a much longer delay time (20 ms)
-- Sometimes the signal is delayed multiple times.
-- Those delays are modulated, causing some pitch shifting and interference.
-- This makes it sound like there is a whole group of signals rather than just one.
- Phaser
-- Dry signal is blended with a signal that is sent through several phase shifts.
-- The phase shifts cause comb filtering in only certain frequency ranges.
-- The phase shifts are modulated, causing whooshing, but different timbre from flanger.
- Phaser Controls
-- Poles: How many phase shifts
-- Rate: How quickly the phase shifts are modulated.
-- Depth: How much the phase shifts are modulated.
-- Feedback: How much of the phasered signal is sent back into itself for more phasing.
- Ring Modulation
-- Input signal is multiplies by a carrier signal, and the result is awesome.
-- Frequency: The frequency of the carrier signal.
-- Depth: Sets how much of the carrier signal is multiplied.
-- Some Ring Mod effects allow modulation of the carrier signal.

Audio and Music Synthesis
- MIDI
-- Musical Instrument Digital Interface
-- Not audio but digital instructions in the form of binary code.
-- MIDI messages contain 2-3 bytes of information.
-- Bytes in MIDI messages classified as either status bytes or data bytes.
- Status Bytes
-- Specify the MIDI channel (1-16) and the message type
-- Types of MIDI messages:
--- Note on
--- Note off
--- Pitch Bend Change
--- Control Change 
--- Etc...
- Data Bytes
-- A number between 1-127
-- Used as the actual data for the message
- MIDI Controllers
-- Something that generates MIDI
-- Examples: Keyboard, Drum Pads, EWI, Pitch tracking devices, Etc...
- MIDI Flexibility
-- It's just data so it can control anything.
-- Synthesizer's/samplers. Lights/Smoke machines. Console Automation. Etc...
- Velocity: How hard someone is pressing a note.
- Synthesizers and Synthesis
-- Creates vibrations like any instrument
-- Vibrations in the form of an electrical signal
-- Signal able to be modified in ways that are not possible with acoustic instruments.
-- Modular vs. All-In-One.
-- Analog vs Digital
--- Analog synths use analog circuitry, digital synths use digital signal processing.
--- Certain types of synthesis are only feasible with digital.
- Waveforms
-- Synths use alternating currents to create electrical signals.
-- Above Unity is pushing on the speaker cone, below Unity is pulling on it.
-- The shape of the waveform determines the timbre of the sound.
- Harmonics
-- The reason sound waves sound different is because they have different harmonics.
-- A harmonic of is just a multiple of a fundamental frequency. 
- Components of Synthesis
-- Oscillator: Generates the original waveform.
-- Amplifier: Controls loudness pertaining to certain parameters.
-- Envelope: Helps shape the waveform playback.
-- Filter: Helps shape sound by filtering out unwanted frequencies. Essential to subtractive synthesis.
-- FX
- Subtractive Synthesis
-- Older, analog synthesizers.
-- Start with Waveform or noise, then subtract away until desired sound is achieved.
-- Use filters to subtract away.
-- Cutoff Frequency.
- Additive Synthesis
-- Any sound can be made by combining a series of sine waves at various frequencies.
-- Most sounds in nature are complex. 100s if not 1000s of sine waves to be accurately reproduced.
-- Any types of waves can be mixed to create new, interesting sounds.
- FM Synthesis
-- Frequency Modulation
-- Two Waves:
--- Carrier: What is heard
--- Modulator: Controls the pitch of the carrier.
-- Usually only implemented in digital synths.
- Granular Synthesis
-- Chopping sound up into very small pieces (called grains)
-- If you chop up a sound, and do different processing on each piece, you can make unique sounds.
-- Pitch shifting, looping, filtering, overlapping with other grains, playback speed of groups of grains, playback direction.

Connections, Speakers, and More Sound Effects
- Connection Types
-- XLR
--- Commonly used for microphones, powered speaker connections, and long cable runs.
--- Carry a balanced signal.
--- Three Pins: Positive (Hot), negative (Cold), and ground.
--- Male end - pins, female end - holes
-- 1/4 inch TS (Tip/Sleeve)
--- Used for instruments and powered speaker connections
--- Carry an unbalanced signal
--- Two Configurations:
---- Instrument: Tip is signal, sleeve is ground.
---- Speaker: Tip and sleeve both get signal.
--- Susceptible to noise.
-- 1/4 inch TRS (Tip, Ring, Sleeve)
--- Used for connection audio gear and stereo applications
--- Two Configurations:
---- Balanced (Mono): Tip and ring received balanced signal, sleeve is the ground.
---- Stereo: Tip gets left, ring gets right, sleeve is ground. Two signal are unbalanced.
--- Mono Configuration is less noisy than TS.
-- 1/8 TRS (aka 3.5 mm)
--- Used in stereo configuration for headphones mostly.
--- Not typically used in balanced configuration.
--- Commonly seen in Y converter cables with two mono RCA connectors on the other end.
-- RCA (Radio Corporation of America)
--- Typically used for consumer analog or digital audio signals (s/pdif)
--- Pin is the signal, the shell is the ground.
--- Used for short cable runs as they can be very noisy.
- Speakers
-- Two main types of speakers.
-- Important Speaker Terminology
--- Driver: The thing making the sound
--- Tweeter: High Frequency driver
--- Woofer: Low frequency driver
--- Subwoofer: ultra low frequency driver.
--- Crossover: circuitry that determines which frequencies go to which driver.
--- Port: piping in the box that increases low frequency response.
--- Amplifier: adds gain to the signal so the drivers can actually move air.
-- Speaker Classifications
--- Powered: Contains amplifier inside of speaker enclosure.
--- Unpowered: Requires an external amplifier. Does not need to be plugged into a power outlet directly.
--- Active/Passive: Differences in crossover design. Active is less prone to distortion.
--- Bi-Amped: Separate amplifiers power the tweeter and woofer.
