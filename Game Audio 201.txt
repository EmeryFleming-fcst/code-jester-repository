Section 3: Stereo Recording Techniques, Phase, BIG Combat Sounds
- Phase
-- A particular point in the cycle of a waveform, measured as an angle.
-- Plays a huge role when combining waveforms.
-- Interference
--- The way in which two waveforms interact
--- Positive Interference
---- An increase in amplitude.
--- Negative Interference
---- A decrease in amplitude.
--- Interference is negligible in waveforms that are not nearly identical.
--- Complex waveforms make the effects more timbrel than volume.
-- Phase and Interference in the DAW and Studio
--- DAW sources:
---- Duplicating a track
---- Polarity switch. Cancels a sound.
---- Delay.
---- Parallel Processing
--- Studio Sources:
---- Two mics picking up the same source (3:1 rule)
---- Standing Waves
---- Incorrect polarity of speakers and mics.
--- 3:1 Rule
---- When recording with multiple mics, the second mic must be at least 3 times the distance away from a sound source than the first mic.
---- Exceptions:
----- Stereo Pair (Treat the pair as a single mic)
----- Drum Set
- Stereo Recording Techniques
-- Two mics can provide us with a stereo recording.
-- Must be set up to avoid phase cancellation issues.
-- Stereo Microphone: Has two transducers that record in stereo.
-- Stereo Pair: Two Microphones, typically of the same model, set up to record in stereo.
-- Matched Pair: Two microphones of the same model with adjacent serial numbers meant to be used together as a stereo pair.
-- X/Y
--- Two coincident cardioid mic capsules at an angle of 90 degrees
--- Smallest stereo image with the least phase cancellation and the most mono compatibility.
--- Use in a bad sounding room or if end user will likely have a system that plays back in mono or close to it
-- ORTF
--- Two capsules 17 cm apart at a 110 degree angle.
--- Simulates placement of ears, similar to Binaural.
--- Wider image than X/Y
--- Little phase cancellation and good mono compatibility.
--- Use in nicer sounding rooms and for large groups.
-- Blumlein Pair
--- Two coincident, crossed, figure 8 capsules.
--- Wider image than X/Y.
--- Little Phase Cancellation and good mono compatibility.
--- Lots of Room Sound.
--- Use for very natural sounding stereo recording in good sound rooms.
