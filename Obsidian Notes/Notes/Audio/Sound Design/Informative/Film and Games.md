### Mono VS Stereo Effects [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7lVV7Gww-Vg&)
- Music is usually Stereo.
- Ambiences are Stereo.
- Dialogue is typically Mono.
- Foley is typically Mono.
- Sound Effects can be both.
- If the sound is 2D it can be both, if it has 3D space it is always going to be mono.
- Some sounds can have parts that are both via layers.
### How Producers Layer Sounds [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aURQtHHQf18)
- Complimenting: Layering similar sounds that will work with another sound to add something the other sound is lacking. Creating the feeling of one sound.
- Contrasting: Adding a sound to another sound that does something different to create texture or push against it to create Contrast. User can identify several sounds.
- Ask yourself first: What am I lacking? What am I missing? What will enhance it?
### The Secret to Layering Any Sound Perfectly [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-nVbREJkQ4)
- One Main Layer, the rest add Texture. Use EQ to fit them together.
- Pan Layers creates a wide and dense sound.
- Make sure every layer has a purpose
### Sound Design - Layering [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4RP6sP-UfpQ)
- Most Real World sounds are little symphonies. When you do this kind of layering right, they combine to create new sounds that are unrecognizable, but still familiar enough to be accepted as real. Think about all the things that would be happening in the object you're trying to create, and then which individual sound-making objects work the same way.
- Each kind of layer should be responsible for a different part of the spectrum.
- "Offset layering will allow you to produce richer and more complex sounds from single sources, giving depth and fullness to an otherwise boring sound. It can also create complex sources for ambiences, science fiction, water, and other sounds"
