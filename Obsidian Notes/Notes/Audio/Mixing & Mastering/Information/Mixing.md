### Tips for Pro Mixes [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PUYdVKIWJkQ)
1. Normalize anywhere from -10db to -20db
2. Volume Faders exhibit higher resolution near a nominal 0db setting, offering finer control over adjustment.
3. Use Clip gain automation for vocals to make compression more natural.
4. Choose an anchor to volume balance around the rest of your tracks. For example, a kick at 8-10db.
5. Lower clip gain for De-Essing. Doing this before De-Essing ensures the plugin does not need to work as hard.
6. Other Tips for De-Essing
	1. Layers of De-essing. 
	2. Find the exact frequency and cut it out of just that one vocal. Pitch Down.
7. Add notes to tracks
	1. In Ableton, Edit Info Text of a Track. Right Click Timeline, Add Locator.
8. Parallel Compression
	1. Applying heavy compression to a copy of a signal and then blending that copied signal into the original signal. 
	2. Gives greater control of shaping a sound and allowing it to cut through a dense mix. 
	3. Carve out midrange and boost lows and highs of copy as well. 
	4. Essentially feeds in power and energy (low and high). Can also automate mute to make it only work for certain sections.
9. Rear Bus Compression
	1. Parallel compress everything in the mix aside from the drums and possibly the vocals. It acts as a subtle enhancement.
10. Parallel EQ. 
	1. Shape tone with Analog simulation EQ before other EQs.
11. Parallel Saturation can be used on anything. 
12. Take intentional breaks when you are mixing. Sit back and recalibrate.
13. Listen & Takes Notes After Each Break
	1. Can do it on a separate audio track armed to take your recordings.
14. Pre-delay to make reverb punchier. 
	1. Use an actual delay plugin.
	2. When sounds are further away, we hear the dry and wet signal of Reverb more meshed together at the same time (Pre-delay). 
	3. No Pre-delay emulates this. Can be used to place instruments to the front or back of your mix.
15. Use Sample Delays for Width
	1. Exceptionally effective for enhancing Percussive Elements that occupy the higher end of the frequency spectrum. 
	2. Haas Effect. 
	3. Effectively making a copy and panning one hard left and the other hard right. Needs to be less than 40ms.
16. Chiaroscuro
	1. The contrast between light and dark that creates the illusion of depth and realism in art and adds to the emotional experience of the viewer
		1. Panning allows us to create contrast in the width dimension. 
			1. LCR Panning: Hard Left, Hard Right, Dead Center. 
		2. Depth allows sounds to feel further away by contrasting them with sounds that are closer.
17. Understand how EQ affects height. The lower the frequency, the lower the height. The higher the frequency, the higher the height. "Chakras" are activated via frequencies.
18. Use EQ to create depth in the mix. 
19. The further away sounds get, the darker in tone they get. This is why Reverbs often have a built-in high cut filter. 
	1. The essences of a great mix lies in the artful staggering of your sounds through the right EQ adjustments. 
	2. To ensure an instrument stands out while maintaining it's unique place, it's incredibly effective to selectively limit it's frequency range.
20. Low Cut + Low Shelf + Low Mid Cut
21. Abbey Road Reverb Trick
	1. Roll off the low end the high end of your reverb to a desired place. 
	2. High End: Darken the Reverb to a degree that is desired to push the sound back in the mix.
22. De-Esser Before Reverb to get rid of splashiness.
23. Delay after reverb to give it more width and depth.
24. Tremolo after Reverb.
	1. Longer sustained instruments and sounds. Similar to Ping Pong Effect except you can control depth.
25. Compressing your Reverb:
	1. Downward or Upward. 
26. Automate the Reverb.
	1. Up in the chorus, for example. Stops the reverb from being too powerful or too weak.
27. Three Reverb Buckets: 
	1. Vocals, Drums, Other. 
	2. Create contrasting areas of depth. 
	3. Use a different reverb for each of the 3 main instrument groups. 
	4. For each instrument bucket, create a long and short version of the reverb. 
		1. Long Reverb is at least 1 second, anything less is short. 
	5. Intentionally apply any other tone or character shaping to the reverbs (EQ, Delay, Etc.). Some can be more narrow than others, some can be darker.
28. Create Contrast with Wide and Narrow Reverbs
	1. As sounds are perceived to be further away, they become more narrow in the stereo field. 
	2. Sounds that are closer to listeners have a more direct path to their ears, making it so they can be more distinctly placed. 
	3. As sounds get further away, the direct sound decreases in volume and the reverberation volume increases. The reflections start to fill the stereo field. 
	4. High frequencies are absorbed more quickly by the air then low frequencies. To create distance, roll off high end, increase decay time, and narrow stereo field.
29. Organize Your Plugins!!!!! 
	1. Ex. Amps and Pedals, Delay, Distort/Clip/Saturate, EQ, Filter, Imaging, Metering, Modulation/Chorus, Multi Effects, Pitch, Preamp/Channel Strip, Reverb, Specialized, Utility
30. It's correct, If That's how you or the client likes it! 
	1. Taste is everything. "It's important that you understand the delineation between the correct way to do something and whether your like it or not." 
	2. The only real rules that exist in audio are those that exist within the physics of acoustics. If it sounds good, it is good.
31. Know your room!!! 
	1. No matter how accurate and controlled you get your room to sound, you're never going to get mixes to translate unless you spend adequate time learning that room. Test your music on speakers outside of the studio. 
	2. Listen to references. Listen to those references before mixing (2-5 Tracks). 
	3. Car Checks. Low end frequencies tend to travel right out of the window. They can go through thin material easily.
32. How to find the sweet spot in your studio 
	1. Harmon Room Mode Calculator. 
		1. Alt. Measure 33% length of your room and use that as the starting point. Square rooms are very difficult. 
	2. Possibly use headphones instead. 
	3. Anything Sonarworks recommends for $200 on their website is reliable. 
	4. Set speakers in an equilateral triangle from that position. At least 45 inches apart. Room EQ Wizard for testing. Aim for a Frequency Response that is +-6 db of Flat and a decay times less than 200ms below 200HZ frequencies. 
	5. SPL Meter helps you find where problem frequencies are in your room, which is where you place your acoustic treatment.
33. Pan Two Different Tonal Reverbs Left and Right. 
	1. Keep the Decay's time similar. Works well with swelling sounds, like Synths and Pads.
34. Pan Reverb/Delay to one side, dry to the other.
35. Volume Automate the end of the phrase to create depth.
36. Clipping instead of Compression for Rhythmic Transients
	1. Preserves headroom and makes it brighter by adding a bit of distortion.
37. Side-chain compression for kick drum. 
38. Side-Chain Reverb Ducking. 
	1. Allows the vocals to momentarily reduce they reverb as the come in, and then release once they cease, ensuring the vocals pierce through the reverb, maintaining their clarity and forward position in the mix.
39. Long Dark Reverb
	1. EQ Feeding Into Reverb Feeding Into Echo. Carve out high and low ends far. Reverb also EQs the lows and highs further. Short Decay time. Add wide delay with 1/4th delay time.
40. Understand How EQ Adds to Transients
	1. EQ Before Clipper makes it "louder" and punchier, even if it doesn't sound like it in solo. It keeps headroom.
41. Reference Stems From Popular Songs
	1. SPLICE, LOOPMASTERS, SKIO MUSIC AND METAPOP, BANDCAMP, SOUNDCLOUD, PRODUCER/ARTIST WEBSITES, PRODUCELIKEAPRO, PRODUCEATHON COMMUNITY, LABEL PROMOTIONS via Social Media or Website
42. Make Friends with Phase
	1. The timing relationship between two waves at any point in time. 
	2. Constructive and Destructive Interference.
	3. Easy solution can be to invert the phase, though sometimes you have to manually align.
43. The Right Way to Hi-Pass
	1. Go too far then drag it back slowly in the context of the mix.
44. Boost the Fundamental of Kick & Snare into a Clipper for maximum punch.
45. Use Dynamic and Static EQ
46. Fletcher Munson Curve and EQ
	1. Ears perceive frequencies in the 1-5k range to be much louder, even when played at the same volume.
	2. Perceived volume differs from actual volume. 
	3. Also called the Equal Loudness Contours. 
	4. Magic of great mixes is in the midrange.
47. When using a reference track, you can narrow the frequency bands through EQ to better match your track to it.
48. Different EQ for different sections of the song: Can automate EQ cuts/boosts.
49. Tilt EQ is useful for quickly brightening/darkening sounds.
50. Adopt a "Fix-first" mindset. 
	1. Before you can enhance a track, you must first fix the issues. 
	2. Sonic and Dynamic Spectrum. E
	3. Q/Volume adjustment, compression, clipping, and limiting. 
	4. Enhancements injects the mix with tone and character and can range from subtle to bold. Akin to painting on a blank canvas.
51. How to hear problems? 
	1. Ear training and practice. "What's just as important as being able to identify a problem is the ability to identify when there are no problems or issues to address." 
	2. When Search and Destroying, don't look for problems. Identify that they exist, and THEN find them. When boosting, never boost more than 12 decibels.
52. Tremolo On High End creates width. 
	1. Depth determines how much of the other end it gets. If 50%, the starting end will have more than the alternating end. 
53. Compression for Depth
	1. Has a great ability to shape the front-end transient on a snare.
54. Know your Compressor! 
	1. Different compressor types have different characters. Especially around Attack and Release.
55. Mixing Mindset When Recording and Producing
	1. Songwriting and Arrangement. Think about how each element sits in the mix. A well-recorded track needs less cleaning in a final mix. 
	2. Select sounds that compliment one another and fill the frequency spectrum. If all of these steps are done with care, a significant portion of the mixing process can be done with just volume faders.
56. Modulation in Reverb
	1. "Depth in Reverb + Rate" Can smooth sounds out and be used for creative effects.
57. Chorus After Reverb
	1. Create additional spaciness.
58. Transient Shaping with Compression
	1. Shape and change release times.  
59. Correlation Meter
	1. Don't overstress about Phase! As long as it's above 80% of the time, it's fine. Centering other instruments helps offset it.
60. Consider Limiters over Compressors
	1. Fast attack and release make it very transparent in how it cuts off transients.
61. Console Emulation
	1. Putting them on your tracks is essentially summing. Adds color. How hot you drive the sound into it determines the color. 8 channels or more is when you really start to notice the sound.
62. Know the different saturation types.
63. Parallel Distortion on the Mix-Bus.
64. Tape Saturation to get rid of Harshness: Tape is great at softening.
65. Phase Invert to hear the difference
66. Parallel Exciter on Vocals
67. Attenuation Select EQP1A
68. Low-Pass on Vocals
	1. Don't be afraid to use aggressive filtering on vocals that need it.
69. Drum Replacement/Layering
	1. Create replacement/double drums if you receive a bad track
70. 808 Saturation + Chorus + Limiting
	1. Focus now sets everything below the frequency threshold Mono.
71. When to Kick the Kick out of the drum bus
72. Bus Compression (VCA Compression) on drum bus to glue them together.
73. Short Ambience on Percussion
	1. Helps create separation and depth
74. Short Decay/Ambience on Backing Vocals
	1. Blend it in slightly to give it depth behind the lead vocal. 
75. How to Mid/Side
76. Contrast in Action
	1. The difference between light and dark is what makes light feel brighter. 
	2. Restrict the three dimensions of a mix to have an impact of power and expansion. Narrow in the stereo-field of a few instruments.
	3. Automate the closing and opening of that element of the mix. 
	4. An example would be to have it be narrow before the chorus and widen when it switches to it.
77. Linear Phase High-Pass on Kick
78. Mix with the Master in Mind
	1. Mindset. Two things involved with mastering, the Sonic Spectrum and the Dynamic Spectrum. 
	2. Sonic Tools: EQ and Saturation. 
	3. Dynamic Tools: Compressors, Clippers, and Limiters. 
	4. Keep the end in mind while mixing. Frequency imbalance and Peaks are the two largest enemies to loudness.
### How To Mix Like A Pro [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1BLZGe-TqW0)
- Song Writing and Arrangement -> Recording -> Production -> Mixing -> Mastering
- Mixing enhances the Dynamic, Sonic, and Spatial Qualities of Music.
- The Sonic Spectrum: 20hz -> 20kHz
- Frequencies are vibrations/pitch.
- Dynamic Spectrum
	- Difference between loudest and quietest parts of the music. 
	- Volume Fade, Compression, Clipping, and Limiting.
- Spatial Dimension
- Give a feeling of space and realism
- Reverb and Delay.
- The technical is there to serve the creative.
- The Goal of Mixing
	- Blend the individual tracks containing the various instruments and sounds in a way that creates a polished, unified, and pleasing auditory experience for the intended listener.
	- How to achieve the right Volume Balance in the mix.
	- How to manipulate Frequencies in the Mix
	- How to manipulate Dynamics in the mix.
	- How to manipulate the Spatial Aspects of the mix.
- The Editing Process:
	- Tuning
	- Timing
	- Cleanup
	- Digital Cleaning is NO GOOD.
- Session Setup and References
	1. Build a New Session: Match Sample Rate, Set Project Tempo, Increase Buffer Size, Import Multi-Tracks and References. 512 Buffer Size for Mixing, Lower for Recording.
	2. Organize the Tracks.
	3. Identify Phase and Polarity Issues.
	4. Noise Gate Unwanted Bleed.
	5. Gain Stage Everything. 
		1. -10 to -20dbfs per track.
		2. Occasional Peak no higher than -6dbfs.
- Parallel Track 
	- Refers to Any Duplicate Copy of a track which has FX on it. 
	- The Parallel (Effected) track can be blended in with the original signal to taste.
- Use Utility Plugin Phase Invert on tracks to see if they are out of phase (Less Punchy)
- Select All. Normalize Region Gain. Peak. -10.
- Volume Balance
	- Adjusting the volume faders of the individual tracks in order to achieve a balanced and cohesive static mix, prior to any processing.
- Fundamental Frequency
	- The frequency of any sound where the energy or volume of that energy is greatest.
- A static mix is one with only volume balance.
- Can play with gain automation when volume balancing. Start with leading role and pair it with the rest.
- Can also pan.
- Frequencies (EQ)
	- Refer to the specific pitches or tones in a sound, spanning from low to high.
	- Frequencies are the height of a mix, Width is panning and Stereo Imaging, Depth is Compression, Reverb, Delay, and EQ.
	- Identify Problems with EQ
		- Low End Muddiness 
			- 20-60hz: Sub-Bass Range
			- 60-250hz: General Bass Range.
		- Mid-Range Clutter
			- 250-500hz: Muddiness or Thickness
			- 500-1khz: "Box", "Honky", "Nasal"
			- 1kHz-3kHz: Intelligibility and Presence. 
		- Harsh Highs
			- 5-8kHz: Sibilance
			- 6-10kHz: Cymbals and High-Pitched Percussion.
	- Fix Sonic Relationships with EQ.
		- Helps reduce Frequency Masking
		- Frequency Carving: Bass & Kick Drum Clarity, Vocal and Guitar Separation, Synth & Snare Definition. 
		- Identify overlapping frequency ranges and make subtle adjustments to create distinct spaces for each element, ensuring clarity and a balanced mix.
		- Boosting with Context
			- Boost while paying close attention to how the boost effects everything.
		- Frequency Slotting
			- Choose which instruments take up which part of the frequency spectrum.
	- Use EQ to bring Global Balance.
		- Keep in mind Frequency Distributions and Profiles.
		- Track Level
		- Bus Level
		- Boosts are typically more broad than cuts are. Broad > 3.0Q.
	- Use EQ to Enhance each Instrument.
		- The more subtle and final moves we make.
		- Analog EQs can sound better on the high end.
- Spatial Processing
	- Reverb simulates the natural echoes that envelop sounds in real environments.
	- Delay creates echoes that can add rhythm, depth, & texture. Further enhancing the spatial illusion.
	- Depth and Width Dimensions.
		- Width: By panning the echoes of the reverb tails we can create a sense of spaciousness. Can add delay to that reverb further enhancing the width. 
	- Delay occupies less space in the mix. 
	- Delays
		- Delay Time: Length of time between the original signal and the echo.
		- Feedback: The number of repeats or echoes.
		- Wet/Dry: Balance between
		- Tap Tempo: Set Delay time by tapping a key.
		- Modulation: Adds Movement and Texture to the echoes.
		- Filter/EQ: Shape Tonal Quality of Delay Signal
		- Stereo Spread/ Panning
		- Ducking: Reduces the volume of the delay when the original signal is present.
	- Analog and Tape Delay: Known for their warm, decaying echoes. Depth without muddiness.
	- Digital Delay: Pristine repeats with precise control. Great for rhythmic patterns.
	- Ping Pong Delay: Back and Forth
	- Slapback Delay: A single short echo with a quick delay time. Mimics a soundwave bouncing off a nearby surface. Tight, energetic feel.
- More Tools and Techniques
	- Saturation
		- The addition of harmonic content that wasn't originally there in a dry signal.
		- Adds this above the fundamental. Because our ears are more sensitive to the 1-3k range it makes it louder.
		- Distortion is heavy saturation.
		- Saturation can often tame the high end, especially in tape machines.
	- Parallel Processing (Compression, Saturation, EQ)
		- Blending in the dry signal with the wet signal.
		- Send at Unity Gain to another bus. Blend it using the volume knob.
	- Sidechain Compression
		- Duck certain instruments out when other instruments need to be more prominent. Fast Attack.
		- If you want to make it transparent, no more than 5db.
	- Dynamic EQ
		- Suppress a certain frequency only when it crosses a certain threshold. Soothe 2.
	- Multiband Compression
		- Take a specific frequency and compress it.

### Simple Mixing Workflow [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aKrF2dra1S0)
1. Session Prep
	- Organize Tracks, Group similar tracks together, Color-coding, Labeling song sections, Gain Staging, Selecting Reference Tracks, Setting Buffer Size to it's highest limit.
2. Initial Balance
	- Faders and Panning. Get the best mix you can with just those.
3. Mix Bus
	- Add effects to here first.
4. Process Top down
	- Process Parent busses first, then process each individual channel as needed.
	- How is the tone? -> How are the dynamics? -> Does it need space?
5. Listen throughs
	- Listen through everything, beginning to end. Take notes on what needs to be fixed. Make Revisions. 3-5 Rounds.
6. Evaluate Translation
	- Make sure it sounds well on any system.
	- Use a Frequency Analyzer to check how closer yours is to the reference.
	- Add EQ at the end of Mix Bus Chain to nudge it as close as you can.
	- Choose any 3 Speaker Systems and play your mix in a playlist next to the reference track. Note any red flags. Phone, Bluetooth Speaker, Earbuds, etc.
