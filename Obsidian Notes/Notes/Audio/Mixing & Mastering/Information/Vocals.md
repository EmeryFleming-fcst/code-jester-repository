### Your Vocals Are Too Loud [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XWskyKzGvpc)
- Step 1: Create Brightness 
	- Sounds that are brighter sound closer to the ear. Shelf on High End.
- Step 2: Create Consistency 
	- Compression. Fast Attack, Slow Release, High Threshold.
	- Serial Compression: One compressor after the other with a lower threshold to smooth it down.
- Step 3: Get Rid of Masking
	- Masking: The perception of one sound is affected by the presence of another. Happens if they have a similar frequency.
	- Mute Button Method: 
		- Mute your instruments one at a time.
		- Listen to the vocal, not the instruments.
		- Ask yourself: Is the vocal suddenly louder or more present?
		- If not, then there's no masking between those two tracks.
		- If so, then masking is occurring.
		- Cut with EQ
- Step 4: Volume Balancing
	- Set your vocal level, set it down a few DB. Forces you to create perceived loudness.
### How To Compress Vocals [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Og8tYrFWazE)
1. Prep the Tracks before compression
	- Clip gain automation early on.
2. Remove Unwanted Frequencies
3. Tame the Peaks (First Compressor)
4. Add Further Consistency (Second Compressor). Use Make-up Gain
5. Add Excitement (Third Compressor). Slow attack time
6. Add Character. This is done through compressor types.
	- Examples: 
		- FET for peak
		- OPTO for consistency
		- FET for excitement.
### Vocal Mixing Chain Order [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xpaAijC4By0)
1. Gain plugin to automate volume
2. Subtractive EQ to tame unwanted frequencies.
3. Compression to Address the Dynamics.
4. Optional Saturation (This can be moved around)
5. Optional De-Esser
6. Additive EQ to boost the pleasant frequencies.
7. Optional second Compression to create more consistency.
8. Third Compression to add Excitement. This should have a slow attack time.
9. Space and Depth (Reverb and Delay)
### 1000 Hours of Vocal Mixing Tutorials in 30 Minutes [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UBigaeYW0mE)
- Compression
	- More makes it up-front at the cost of losing energy. Less makes it further away but it has more energy. Gain staging helps reduce this cost.
- Reverb
	- You can automate it to come in on empty spaces.
- Delay
	- Better alternative to reverbs in a busy mix. You can unsync the delay from the BMP if you want to hear it better.
	- Tip: Put it before a reverb.
		- High Pass it to pull it up.
		- Low Pass it to pull it down. 
		- Make sure it's out of the way of the lead vocals.
- Flanger
	- Adds width. Very helpful if you did not record doubles or triples.
- Formant Shifter
	- Changes the sound of the voice without touching it.
	- Melodyne includes this.
- Distortion
	- Heavier form of saturation that can add grit and energy to a vocal.
- Sampling Vocals
	- Take a portion of the lead vocal, put it on a new track, then put it into a sampling program. Slice it. Apply sensitivity down to the transients. Can now play with Midi.
- LFO
	- Can be used to emulate side chains or rhythmic stuttering.
- Creating a Vocal Swell
	- Take the first part of a lead vocal.
	- Drag it to a new track.
	- Turn on reverb and crank it up.
	- Bounce the track in place.
	- Extend the tail.
	- Reverse the track.
- Be creative with your bussing!
- Doubles and Triples
	- Record the same part multiple times. Pan them hard left and hard right. Add a bit of EQ (Usually High Shelf) and make them more tuned.
	- More Energy can be achieved through recording the other vocal parts an octave up or an octave down.
- Mixing Vocals
	- Mix into a limiter. 
	- Mix in Mono (Music only) at really low volumes.
	- Don't Solo Vocals.
- Send all your instruments into an aux channel then sidechain it with the vocals. Dips out some range.
- Recording
	- Closer you are, the boomier it is. 
	- The further you are, the thinner it is.
	- Record to a drum beat, not a metronome.
