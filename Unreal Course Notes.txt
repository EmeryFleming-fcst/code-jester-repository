And then: Final Project
I'd give them a significant amount of time here.

Something like:
Build a small complete game in Unreal Engine.

Not a giant RPG.

A small game that actually reaches a finished state.

It should ideally require:
- Player control
- Gameplay loop
- UI
- Audio
- Visual effects
- At least one enemy or gameplay obstacle
- Level/environment
- Win/lose state
- Basic saving or progression
- Menus
- Polished presentation
- Packaged build
And I'd have them produce a little bit of professional documentation:
- Game concept
- Core gameplay loop
- Controls
- Feature list
- Asset list
- Technical overview
- Known issues
- Final build
That last part is important for CareerTech students because you're teaching them something closer to production, rather than simply "how to use Unreal."
