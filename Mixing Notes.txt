The Language of Music
- Notes and Scales
-- C, D, E, F, G, A, B
-- Bb Eb F# Ab Bb
-- These 12 notes together are an octave.
-- Distance between two notes is an interval. Steps and Tones.
-- Major: W W H W W W
-- Minor: W H W W H W W
- Chords
-- A group of three or more notes played together.
-- Major: Root -> Three Half Steps -> Four Half Steps
-- Minor: Bring one note from the major down a half step.
-- There are 7 of them, but the diminished 7th is rarely played.
- Progressions
-- I ii iii IV V vi vii
-- Capitals are major chords, lowercase are minor.
-- Popular Chord Progressions:
--- I V vii IV
--- vi IV I V
--- I IV V IV
--- ii V I vi
-- Chord Voicings
--- Add notes to the chord to spice them up.
--- Most common note to add is the 7th.
--- A triad is three notes at once.
--- C Major 7th: I iii V vii
--- Dominant 7th: I iii V viib
--- Minor 7th:  I iiib V viib
--- Add the second chord in the scale to any chord in your progression. Called an Add 9.
--- Others: Suspended Chords, Augmented Chord, 6 ninths, etc.
-- Inversions
--- Playing the same note in a chord in a different order to create a different feel and bridge the gap between chord changes.
--- C Major: I iii V
---- 1st Inversion: iii V I
---- 2nd Inversion: V I iii

Creating Chord Progressions
- Put together Chord progressions, chop them up and re-arrange them experimentally.
- Bringing a note in the chord down an octave and layering it makes it sound fuller. Can do this multiple times.
- Can bring notes up and down an octave to minimize the gap between different chord changes.
- Can start to add some extended voices.
- Transposing: Moving up and down keys.

Rhythm
- An event that repeats regularly over time.
- At the heart of a rhythm is a beat.
- A single beat that repeats at regular time intervals is called a pulse.
- To create your own pulse, you can use a metronome. It's measured in beats per minute.
- High and low beat of a metronome. Low beat measures the down beat. 1, 1.1.2, 1.2, 1.2.2, 1.3, 1.3.2 
- Time Signature: 4/4 means there are 4 equal part in 1 bar.
- Can automate BPM in a DAW over time. Rare.
- Can set subdivisions
-- Tempo stays the same. You just increase the number of notes that can live within that one bar in the BMP.
- Triplets
-- Changes the notes
-- Rather than 4 notes in a bar, you have three.
-- Can add different parts on different notes. Kick on the 4th, snare on the 8th, Hi-Hat on the 16th.
-- 4 bars are a phrase

3 Ways to Create Beats
- Sampler
-- Put a sound in the sampler and play it on the MIDI track.
-- Usually you play around with the attack and release.
- Rack & Kit
-- Several different sounds mapped to different keys.
-- Drum Kit: Kick, Snare, Hi-Hats, etc.
-- You can create your own racks.
-- You can use loops to create your own.
- Step Sequencer
-- Activating different pre-configured sounds on bars.
- Drag and Drop Audio
-- Dropping Loops directly into a track and editing them.
-- Hot Key For Muting
-- Find setting for snap settings

- EMPRE system
-- Environment
--- Choose your Room
---- Noise
----- Control background noise.
----- Check Equipment.
---- Reflection
----- Pick a less reflective room.
----- Tame the reflective surfaces.
----- Use acoustic panels, blankets, mattresses, or pillows.
---- Resonances
----- Avoid Corners
----- Avoid the center of your room
-- Microphones
--- Dynamic
---- More forgiving of noisier environments.
--- Condenser
---- Will pick up more detail and have a wider frequency response.
-- Positioning your Mic
--- Isolation Station
--- The 1/2 over Rule
--- Pop Filter Please
--- The 5 inch rule. Mark a distance from the mic.
--- The Proximity Factor
---- 7 - 12 inches away from the mic
--- Eyes for highs Chest for Depth
---- Aim around the eye for highs.
---- Aim around the upper chest for depth.
---- Aim at mouth for neutral.
-- Recording your Takes
--- Get Comfy
---- Create an inviting space
---- Experiment with what works for you
--- Get Levels
---- Keep it 90% green looking at the audio interface.
---- -12 to -18dbfs, no higher than -10dbfs.
--- Follow a Workflow
---- Ride the Creative Momentum in the Room
---- Option 1: 3-5 Full takes and punch in sections as needed.
---- Option 2: Record 3-5 Takes section by section
---- Option 3: Record High and Low Energy Parts together.
--- Focus on Performance
---- Allow the singer to dial in performance energy.
---- Play around with facial expressions.
---- Create a Great Environment to explore.
-- Editing
--- Comping
---- Taking the best pieces and putting it together.
---- How to Comp
----- Listen through each take one at a time.
----- Choose the best parts and compile them into a master take.
----- Crossfade any cuts for smooth transitions.
--- TNT
---- Timing
----- Listen to the vocal in the context of the music/groove.
----- Don't make it too perfect.
----- Time compression and time stretching when there's issues within the phrase itself.
---- Noise and Clean Up
----- Mouth noises. Breaths. Sibilants. Consonants. Etc.
------ Breath removals and volume are stylistic.
------ Remove things from doubles and harmonies if they're distracting. Clicks and breaths.
---- Tuning
----- Manual Pitch Correction
----- Auto Pitch Correction
----- Listen in the context of the mix
----- Auto Pitch Correction after
--- Gain
---- Normalize: Peak at -10
---- Gain Automation
----- Get things about as even as you can. Both loud and quiet.
----- You only really do this on the lead vocal.
